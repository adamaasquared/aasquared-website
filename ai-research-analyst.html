Case Study

From 10 hours to 20 minutes: building my own AI research analyst

Weekly research time

Before: compiling it by hand 10+ hours
↓
After: reading the Monday brief 20 min

Bars drawn to scale, per week.

When I started building a framework for US companies expanding into Europe, I needed to know which companies were making that move and when. That meant scanning funding news, office openings and hiring signals across hundreds of websites. It took over 10 hours a week, and I was still only scratching the surface.

The problem

Doing it by hand meant the research was always partial and always late. By the time I'd found a company worth contacting, I'd used up the time I should have spent contacting it.

What I built

Using Claude, I set up a scheduled agent that runs every Monday morning. It searches funding, expansion and hiring news for US companies making early moves into Europe, flags the ones that fit my criteria, and writes a short brief that lands in my Gmail drafts. I didn't write code. I designed the prompts, the criteria and the sources, then checked the output against my own judgment week after week.

What broke, and what I changed

It didn't work perfectly from day one.

  • Some companies it flagged didn't fit. One had already been established in the UK for over two years, so it was no use for a framework about first entry into Europe. I tightened the criteria so the agent screens those out.
  • I used the same approach for a second agent that searches for fractional roles every day. It started out returning 20 to 75 roles a day, mostly irrelevant. I narrowed the criteria until the list was short enough to act on, and dropped LinkedIn as a source because the agent couldn't reliably access it.
  • It still needs a human (me). The brief is where I start. I read every one and decide which companies are worth a conversation.

What it changed

The Monday brief now takes me 20 minutes to read instead of over 10 hours to compile, and it covers far more ground than I ever could by hand. Because it runs every week, it also picks up patterns I'd miss. One example: AI-native US companies kept choosing London for their first European base, which told me where to focus my outreach first.

What this looks like for a marketing team

Any marketing team that needs ongoing insight on its market can do the same: competitor launches and pricing, category news, retailer and press coverage, or what people are saying about a brand. Ten hours back each week is more than a full working day for every person doing this by hand. The tool is the easy part. Deciding what good looks like, which sources to trust and when the output can be trusted is marketing judgment, and that's where most AI pilots stall.
